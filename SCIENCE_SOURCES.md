## NASA Science — Mars
- ID: `nasa-mars` · Topic: Planet
- URL: https://science.nasa.gov/mars/
  - Mars is the fourth planet from the Sun.
  - Mars has a thin atmosphere made mostly of carbon dioxide.
  - Mars has two small moons, Phobos and Deimos.
  - Mars gets its red colour from iron oxide (rust) in its dust and rocks.
  - Olympus Mons on Mars is the largest volcano known in the solar system.
  - Surface gravity on Mars is about 38 percent of Earth gravity.
  - A day on Mars (a sol) lasts about 24 hours and 37 minutes.
  - Mars is about half the diameter of Earth.

## NASA Science — Earth’s Moon
- ID: `nasa-moon` · Topic: Moon
- URL: https://science.nasa.gov/moon/
  - The Moon is Earth’s only natural satellite.
  - The Moon is on average about 384,400 km from Earth.
  - The Moon has almost no atmosphere (an extremely thin exosphere).
  - Gravity on the Moon is about one-sixth of Earth gravity.
  - The Moon always shows Earth the same side because it is tidally locked.
  - Twelve astronauts walked on the Moon during NASA’s Apollo programme (1969–1972).
  - Craters on the Moon were formed mostly by impacts of asteroids and comets.

## NASA Science — The Sun
- ID: `nasa-sun` · Topic: Star
- URL: https://science.nasa.gov/sun/
  - The Sun is a star at the centre of our solar system.
  - The Sun is a ball of hot plasma powered by nuclear fusion of hydrogen into helium in its core.
  - Earth is about 150 million km (1 AU) from the Sun.
  - Sunlight takes about 8 minutes to reach Earth.
  - The Sun’s diameter is about 109 times that of Earth.
  - Sunspots are cooler, darker regions caused by magnetic activity.
  - Never look directly at the Sun without proper certified solar filters.

## NASA Science — Venus
- ID: `nasa-venus` · Topic: Planet
- URL: https://science.nasa.gov/venus/
  - Venus is the second planet from the Sun.
  - Venus is the hottest planet in the solar system, with surface temperatures around 465 °C.
  - Venus has a thick carbon dioxide atmosphere with clouds of sulfuric acid.
  - Surface pressure on Venus is about 90 times that on Earth.
  - Venus spins backwards (retrograde) compared with most planets.
  - A day on Venus is longer than its year.
  - Venus is similar in size to Earth.

## NASA Science — Saturn
- ID: `nasa-saturn` · Topic: Planet
- URL: https://science.nasa.gov/saturn/
  - Saturn is the sixth planet from the Sun and a gas giant.
  - Saturn is made mostly of hydrogen and helium and has no solid surface to land on.
  - Saturn’s rings are made of countless pieces of ice and rock.
  - Titan is Saturn’s largest moon and has a thick atmosphere.
  - Saturn is about 9.5 AU from the Sun on average.
  - Saturn has the lowest average density of the planets, less than water.

## NASA Science — Asteroids
- ID: `nasa-asteroids` · Topic: Asteroid
- URL: https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/asteroids/
  - Asteroids are rocky, airless remnants left over from the formation of the solar system.
  - Most known asteroids orbit the Sun in the main belt between Mars and Jupiter.
  - Asteroids range in size from a few metres to hundreds of kilometres.
  - Small asteroids have very weak gravity.
  - Scientists study asteroid samples to learn about the early solar system.
  - Some asteroids are rubble piles: loose collections of rock held together by gravity.

## NASA Science — Comets
- ID: `nasa-comets` · Topic: Comet
- URL: https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/comets/
  - Comets are frozen leftovers from the formation of the solar system made of ice, dust and rock.
  - When a comet comes near the Sun, it heats up and releases gas and dust, forming a coma and tails.
  - A comet’s tails point away from the Sun.
  - Many short-period comets come from the Kuiper Belt; long-period comets are thought to come from the Oort Cloud.
  - The solid centre of a comet is called the nucleus.

## NASA Science — Exoplanets
- ID: `nasa-exoplanets` · Topic: Exoplanet
- URL: https://science.nasa.gov/exoplanets/
  - An exoplanet is a planet outside our solar system, usually orbiting another star.
  - Thousands of exoplanets have been confirmed.
  - The transit method detects an exoplanet by the small dip in a star’s brightness when the planet passes in front.
  - The habitable zone is the range of distances where liquid water could exist on a planet’s surface.
  - Being in the habitable zone does not mean a planet has life.
  - Telescopes can study exoplanet atmospheres by analysing starlight that passes through them (spectroscopy).

## NASA Space Place — What Is a Satellite?
- ID: `nasa-satellites` · Topic: Artificial satellite
- URL: https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/satellite/en/
  - A satellite is any object that orbits a larger object; the Moon is a natural satellite.
  - Artificial satellites are human-made machines launched into orbit.
  - Satellites are used for communication, weather forecasting, navigation and science.
  - Many satellites use solar panels for electrical power.
  - Satellites stay in orbit because they move sideways fast enough that they keep falling around Earth.

## NASA — International Space Station
- ID: `nasa-iss` · Topic: Space station
- URL: https://www.nasa.gov/international-space-station/
  - The International Space Station orbits Earth about every 90 minutes.
  - The ISS orbits roughly 400 km above Earth.
  - Astronauts on the ISS appear weightless because they are in continuous free fall around Earth.
  - The ISS uses life-support systems to provide oxygen, remove carbon dioxide and recycle water.
  - The ISS is a partnership of several space agencies.

## NASA Science — Earth
- ID: `nasa-earth` · Topic: Planet
- URL: https://science.nasa.gov/earth/
  - Earth is the third planet from the Sun and the only place known to have life.
  - Earth’s atmosphere is mostly nitrogen and oxygen.

## NASA Science — Meteors & Meteorites
- ID: `nasa-meteors` · Topic: Meteoroid
- URL: https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/meteors-meteorites/
  - A meteoroid is a small rock or particle in space.
  - A meteor is the streak of light seen when a meteoroid burns up in Earth’s atmosphere.
  - A meteorite is a piece that survives the trip and lands on the ground.

## NASA Science — Our Solar System
- ID: `nasa-solar-system` · Topic: System
- URL: https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/
  - The solar system has eight planets orbiting the Sun.
  - Dwarf planets such as Pluto and Ceres are smaller worlds that have not cleared their orbital neighbourhood.
  - The Kuiper Belt is a region of icy bodies beyond Neptune.
  - The Oort Cloud is a distant, inferred reservoir of icy objects that has not been directly observed.

## NASA Science — Universe
- ID: `nasa-universe` · Topic: Universe
- URL: https://science.nasa.gov/universe/
  - Galaxies are huge systems of stars, gas, dust and dark matter held together by gravity.
  - Black holes are regions where gravity is so strong that not even light can escape.
  - Nebulae are clouds of gas and dust where stars can form.
  - Neutron stars are extremely dense remnants of massive stars; pulsars are rotating neutron stars that emit beams.
  - White dwarfs are the hot, dense remnants of stars like the Sun.
  - Scientists do not know whether the entire universe is infinite.

## NASA Space Place — Auroras
- ID: `spaceplace-aurora` · Topic: Event
- URL: https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/aurora/en/
  - Auroras are lights in the sky caused by charged particles from the Sun interacting with Earth’s atmosphere near the poles.

## NASA Space Place — Eclipses
- ID: `spaceplace-eclipse` · Topic: Event
- URL: https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/eclipses/en/
  - A solar eclipse happens when the Moon passes between the Sun and Earth.
  - A lunar eclipse happens when Earth’s shadow falls on the Moon.

## NASA — James Webb Space Telescope
- ID: `nasa-webb` · Topic: Space telescope
- URL: https://science.nasa.gov/mission/webb/
  - The James Webb Space Telescope observes mainly in infrared light.
  - Webb operates near the Sun–Earth L2 point, about 1.5 million km from Earth.

## NASA — Orbital Debris Program Office
- ID: `nasa-debris` · Topic: Space debris
- URL: https://orbitaldebris.jsc.nasa.gov/
  - Orbital debris is human-made objects in orbit that no longer serve a purpose.
  - Even tiny debris can damage spacecraft because it travels at very high speed.

## Science accuracy rules kept in the game
- The Moon is Earth's natural satellite; artificial satellites are human-made.
- The Sun is a star. Nobody lands on a star, black hole or gas giant in CosmoTrekk.
- Mars has a thin atmosphere, Venus has extreme surface conditions, and Saturn has no solid landing surface.
- Breathable oxygen is not freely available at these destinations, so habitats need engineered life support.
- Alien encounters, the rogue drone, stations, training sites and the Horizon Star Map are Simulation Fiction.
- Potential habitability does not prove life exists, and we do not know whether the entire universe is infinite.
